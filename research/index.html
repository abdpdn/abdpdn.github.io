Research

My research focuses on the numerical study of quantum many-body systems, particularly low-dimensional lattice models in which interactions, disorder, topology, and quantum entanglement give rise to unconventional phases and phase transitions.

Research Topics

I study strongly correlated bosonic, fermionic, and spin systems, with particular emphasis on topological phases and quantum phase transitions. A major part of my work concerns the effects of interactions and quasiperiodic disorder on localization, transport, topology, and many-body dynamics. I am also interested in many-body localization, Thouless pumping, non-Hermitian quantum systems, and the characterization of quantum phases through entanglement, spectral, and topological diagnostics.

Methods

My work is primarily computational. I use density-matrix renormalization group and matrix-product-state methods to study ground-state and dynamical properties of one-dimensional systems. I also employ exact diagonalization, sparse-matrix techniques, cluster mean-field theory, finite-size scaling, and large-scale simulations on high-performance computing clusters.
